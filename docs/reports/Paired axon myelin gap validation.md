# The pairing gap survives, but narrower

As of 12 November 2026 the research gap is still open, but it is narrower than our four working claims say, and two of them would not get past a careful reviewer as written. What still holds: every published axon–myelin pipeline we found decides which axon belongs to which myelin ring with a hand-written rule after the network runs. No method makes a valid pair a property of its output, and no axon/myelin paper trains on g-ratio error (the g-ratio is axon diameter divided by fiber diameter). What no longer holds: "no model predicts axon and ring jointly" is **false**. **AxonPath** (code and models released September–October 2026, preprint still in preparation) is one U-Net with three heads that predicts fiber and axon cues together, and the optic-nerve dual-decoder paper also predicts inner and outer masks from one network. "No dual-radius StarDist exists" is literally true but misleading. Nested polar contours with containment guaranteed by construction already exist for single objects: vessel walls in 2022 and, closest of all, **NPS-Net** (April 2026), which uses exactly r_cup = α·r_disc with α in [0, 1]. Bi-Polar Mask (ICIP 2022) already predicts two polar contours per instance for many cells. The ASTIH result (Dice about 0.97/0.92 but fiber F1 of 0.791) is real, but it measures fiber detection, not pairing, so it motivates the problem without proving it. What we can still defend is a **combination**: dense, many-instance, single-shot prediction of touching ring-shaped objects in which pairing and containment hold by construction, plus a per-instance g-ratio loss under full mask supervision, plus a metric that scores pairing correctness directly. For the two-week plan this means three changes: AxonPath becomes a required baseline, the oracle-pairing experiment becomes the paper's central motivation rather than a side check, and the dual-radius head stays deferred because it is no longer a headline novelty.

## ASTIH's 0.791 measures fiber detection, not pairing

ASTIH (MIDL 2026) does show the core tension. On TEM1, its per-dataset nnU-Net reaches **axon Dice 0.974 and myelin Dice 0.924**, yet once the standard AxonDeepSeg watershed turns these masks into fibers, it gets **precision 0.747, recall 0.851, F1 0.791 at IoU 0.5** ([ASTIH](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mlresearch/v315/main/assets/collin26a/collin26a.pdf)). The problem is what that number counts. ASTIH labels each fiber as axon ∪ myelin, matches predicted and true fibers with `stardist.matching`, and describes the metric as a way "to quantify the ability to localize individual axons" through "object-level detection metrics". It reports no merge or split counts, no pairing-violation count and no g-ratio error. Its only stated limitation is that the watershed "may struggle in densely packed or pathological tissues" ([ASTIH §2.2.3, §4](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mlresearch/v315/main/assets/collin26a/collin26a.pdf)). The ground truth was checked by script so that "every myelin region contained exactly one axon region", but predictions are never checked against that rule.

Three facts weaken the "pairing is unsolved" reading. First, the identical nnU-Net + watershed pipeline scores **F1 0.949 on TEM2**, so the watershed is not uniformly bad. TEM1's gap is dataset-specific: TEM1 includes cuprizone-damaged tissue at 2.36 nm/px, while TEM2 is high-pressure-frozen tissue at 4.93 nm/px ([ASTIH Table 4](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mlresearch/v315/main/assets/collin26a/collin26a.pdf)). Second, the comparison model that does better, Cellpose with **F1 0.882** (precision 0.990, recall 0.797), was trained "on the combined axon and myelin masks" and, in the authors' words, "does not support myelin morphometry". It never separates axon from myelin, so its pairing cannot be wrong by definition. Third, our own reading of Table 4 shows recall of 0.955 at IoU 0.3, so few real fibers are missed outright. The losses come from about 16% extra or fragmented predictions (precision 0.837) and from matched fibers whose overlap falls between 0.3 and 0.5. Pairing failures produce exactly these symptoms. A fragmented axon seeds two fibers, and myelin shared by two touching fibers gets split in the wrong place. But ordinary false positives, which ASTIH's Figure 5 highlights as "spurious predictions", produce them too.

The honest wording is therefore: **"On ASTIH TEM1, near-ceiling compartment Dice (0.974/0.924) drops to 0.791 fiber F1@0.5 once the standard axon-seeded watershed converts masks into fibers. No published benchmark measures whether each axon is paired with its own sheath, and none reports per-fiber g-ratio error."** The size of the pairing share inside the 0.791 is an open, measurable question. That makes it a good first experiment rather than a premise.

## Post-hoc pairing is universal; "no joint prediction" is not

Reading the code and methods sections confirms the core of claim 2. Every tool that outputs paired fibers uses a rule to make the pairs.

**AxonDeepSeg** and the ASTIH baseline seed a watershed at axon centroids and flood the negative axon distance map inside axon ∪ myelin. Through v5.5.0 and commits up to 30 October 2026, ADS still ships no instance model ([ADS commits](https://github.com/axondeepseg/axondeepseg/commits/master)). Won et al.'s rat sciatic-nerve study (*Sci Rep*, July 2026) used an ADS bright-field model "without any additional retraining" and paired each axon "with the nearest myelin sheath using the minimal Euclidean distance" in an ImageJ macro ([Sci Rep](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-026-60192-8)). **AxonID** (VCU, GitHub, October 2026) trains a semantic UNet++ with weighted Dice + cross-entropy and then "pairs each axon with its fiber" by watershed and matched centroids ([AxonID](https://github.com/VCU-Orthopaedics-Microsurgery-Lab/AxonID)). **MicroStructFormer** uses a myelin-barrier watershed and geodesic assignment on top of ADS masks ([bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.05.26.727755v2.full)). **AimSeg** builds a fibre ⊃ inner ⊃ axon hierarchy with seeded rules in ilastik and Fiji ([PMC10691719](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10691719/)). gACSON assigns myelin to axons by watershed and distance ([arXiv 2112.06476](https://arxiv.org/html/2112.06476v2)). **MyeliMetric** (ASN Neuro, December 2025) segments nothing. It pairs axon and myelin by spreadsheet column names and "relies on the accuracy of upstream segmentation" ([PMC12818378](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12818378/)). Searches for Mask R-CNN, YOLO or StarDist applied to myelinated fibers found nothing.

The part that fails is "no model predicts axon and ring jointly". **AxonPath** (Carrillo-Barberà, Bankhead, Williams et al.) "uses a U-Net with three output heads": a semantic head (background / myelin / inner cylinder), a fibre distance head and an axon distance head. These "are split into individual fibres and axons by seeded watershed", producing a Fibre > InnerCylinder > Axon hierarchy ([AxonPath README](https://github.com/paucabar/axonpath)). So one network does predict fiber and axon cues together. The pairing is still a rule. An inner cylinder is assigned to the fibre it overlaps most, but only if at least 90% of it lies inside that fibre; otherwise it is deleted. Fibres left without an axon are merged into a neighbour within 1 px ([label_ops.py](https://github.com/paucabar/axonpath/blob/main/axonpath/utils/label_ops.py); [post_processing.py](https://github.com/paucabar/axonpath/blob/main/axonpath/inference/post_processing.py)). Its loss is Dice + cross-entropy plus mean squared error on the two distance maps, with no g-ratio term ([losses.py](https://github.com/paucabar/axonpath/blob/main/axonpath/utils/losses.py)). Its evaluator scores fibres, inner cylinders and axons as separate object classes at IoU 0.5–0.9, with no pair-correctness score, and no numbers have been published. Models (including an `em_cns` model at 8 nm/px) are on Zenodo under CC-BY ([Zenodo 22982902](https://zenodo.org/records/22982902)), and the QuPath extension says "a preprint describing AxonPath is in preparation" ([qupath-extension-axonpath](https://github.com/paucabar/qupath-extension-axonpath)). Because AxonPath's fibre watershed and axon watershed run independently, mismatches remain possible. An axon can straddle two fibres, an inner cylinder 85% inside its fibre gets dropped, and a fibre can end up with two axons. That is exactly the room a "valid by construction" method needs.

The g-ratio half of claim 2 survives in the domain. Every axon/myelin deep-learning paper checked trains on pixel losses and computes g-ratio afterwards, and a GitHub code search for "g_ratio loss" found nothing related to myelin. This is "not found within our search", not "proven absent". Outside the domain, the idea already exists. Meng et al. (MICCAI 2022) build a **differentiable cup-to-disc ratio from soft areas** (sums of predicted probabilities) and supervise it with mean squared error, for one nested structure pair per eye image ([Meng et al. 2022](https://livrepository.liverpool.ac.uk/3169382/1/MICCAI2022_Shape-Aware%20Weakly_Semi-Supervised%20Optic%20Disc%20and%20Cup%20Segmentation%20with%20Regional_Marginal%20Consistency.pdf)). Kervadec et al. (MIDL 2021 Best Paper) constrain soft size and **ratios of shape descriptors between classes** ([PMLR 143](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v143/kervadec21a.html)). R-Super (MedIA 2026) uses the same "sum soft probabilities inside a region" trick for tumour volume ([arXiv 2608.27668](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27668)). Our planned loss, |√(Â_axon / Â_fiber) − g_true| per fiber, is therefore a **per-instance, many-object version of a known idea**, not a new kind of loss.

## AxonPath, not the optic-nerve paper, is now the closest prior work

Claim 3 named the optic-nerve dual decoder (Ma, Hao et al., *Ophthalmology Science* 2026) as the nearest work. It remains the closest **peer-reviewed** one: two decoders for inner (axon) and outer (fiber) masks, trained with weighted BCE + Dice, reaching **g-ratio R² 0.805** while diameters exceed R² 0.96 ([PMC13091109](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13091109/)). Two details matter for how we cite it. It reports only pixel-level scores (inner F1 0.771 and outer 0.697 in one place, 0.763 and 0.691 in the Results text, an inconsistency inside the paper), no instance metrics and no g-ratio bias. It never says how an inner mask is linked to its outer mask. The only post-processing described is removing small connected components and pruning borders. Its stated code repository returned 404 in our earlier check. AxonPath goes further than this paper on our axis: three coordinated heads, an explicit fibre-to-axon hierarchy, open training code, and a CNS-TEM model. It must be the primary "closest work" in the paper even though it is not yet peer-reviewed.

The rest of the list needs one wording fix. AimSeg, gACSON and MicroStructFormer do segment. They do not "only measure g-ratio"; they compute it after the fact with rule-based pairing. Won et al. retrain nothing and report only group means (for example g 0.56 vs manual 0.54, P = 0.131, over six nerves) ([Sci Rep](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-026-60192-8)). MyeliMetric is purely downstream statistics. Better wording: **"These tools compute g-ratio after segmentation and pairing; none uses it as a training signal or reports per-fiber pairing accuracy."** The g-ratio numbers that do exist support the motivation. AimSeg's g-ratio agreement (CCC 0.75–0.87) trails its fiber-detection F1 of 0.85–0.88 ([PMC10691719](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10691719/)). That repeats the optic-nerve pattern: small boundary errors on two contours compound in a ratio.

## Other fields already have the parts of our design

The method's building blocks each have prior art outside neuroscience. A reviewer from medical imaging or computer vision will know at least some of them.

**Nested polar contours with containment by construction exist, for one object at a time.** Alblas et al. (SPIE Medical Imaging 2022) predict, along 31 rays from a vessel centre, a lumen radius plus a wall thickness, defining r_outer = r_lumen + thickness "so that r_lumen ≤ r_outer wall". They claim an "inductive bias that guarantees ring-shaped vessel walls" ([arXiv 2112.01137](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.01137)). Van Herten et al. use the same "inner radius + positive thickness" form for the heart's left ventricle ([arXiv 2409.11837](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.11837)). The closest is **NPS-Net** (arXiv, April 2026), which writes the cup radius as r_c(θ) = α(θ)·r_d(θ) with α in [0, 1]. It calls α the "angular cup-to-disc ratio" and states "nesting … is preserved" ([arXiv 2604.09062](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.09062)). That is our r_in = σ·r_out with our "per-ray g-ratio" interpretation, almost word for word. All three assume one pre-centred object per crop and use no detection or non-maximum suppression (NMS, the step that removes duplicate detections). NPS-Net also has no ratio loss: its cup-to-disc ratio is used only for evaluation. INBD (CVPR 2023) segments hollow, donut-shaped tree rings by sampling each new boundary outward from the previous one, so nesting holds by construction, but for one concentric family per image ([arXiv 2212.03022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.03022)).

**Two contours per instance for many instances also exist.** Bi-Polar Mask (Zhao & Zhou, ICIP 2022) "utilizes two polars to represent the contours of cell and nucleus" with "a shared center at the nucleus, which allows for straightforward binding of the pair of contours" ([IEEE Xplore](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/9897272)). It is the single biggest unknown in this report. The full text is paywalled, has no open-access copy and no code, so we cannot tell whether nucleus rays are forced inside cell rays. HydraStarDist / HSD-WBR (arXiv 2025) runs two StarDist heads on one shared encoder for nested objects and adds a penalty for inner objects predicted outside outer ones. Containment is encouraged, not guaranteed, and each head runs its own NMS ([arXiv 2504.12078](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.12078)).

**Cell + nucleus tools mostly pair after the fact, as axon tools do.** InstanSeg's code splits its output channels into independent nucleus and cell halves, matches them when the overlap exceeds half the nucleus area, and then overwrites cell pixels with nucleus pixels, which forces containment after the fact ([biological_utils.py](https://github.com/instanseg/instanseg/blob/main/instanseg/utils/biological_utils.py)). Mesmer makes nuclear and whole-cell masks independently and matches them by "maximum overlap" ([PMC9010346](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9010346/)). These are clean contrast cases, and they show the post-hoc pattern is not peculiar to neuroscience.

**"Decide the instance first, then label its parts" is established.** Part-aware panoptic segmentation (CVPR 2021) assigns part labels only to pixels inside an already-decided object, so part-inside-object holds by construction through a top-down merge ([arXiv 2106.06351](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.06351)). Our recommended "instance-first, part-second" decoder applies this recipe; it does not invent it. Containment losses such as TopoInteraction (ECCV 2022) work on semantic classes and are soft penalties ([arXiv 2207.09654](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.09654)).

**Ratio losses help only modestly, and the evidence is confounded.** In Meng et al., adding the ratio loss cut cup-to-disc error from 0.127 to 0.112 MAE, but the loss also brought in about 38,000 extra images labelled only with the ratio, and Dice barely moved ([Meng et al. 2022](https://livrepository.liverpool.ac.uk/3169382/1/MICCAI2022_Shape-Aware%20Weakly_Semi-Supervised%20Optic%20Disc%20and%20Cup%20Segmentation%20with%20Regional_Marginal%20Consistency.pdf)). Shape-descriptor losses used alone lose 0.11–0.22 Dice ([ar5iv 2105.00859](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2105.00859)). A cardiac study found that "excellent scores for DSC and HD do not imply the most optimal volume computation", and that errors partly cancel in the ejection-fraction ratio ([Naidoo et al. 2022](https://repository.uwl.ac.uk/9076/7/A.influence_of_loss_function_on_.pdf)). The same cancellation shows up for nerves: a Dice-trained network matched mean g-ratio to within 0.5% while axon diameter and myelin thickness were off by about 9.5% ([PMC6420354](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6420354)). No study isolates "ratio loss vs plain Dice with identical full mask labels" for nested objects. That ablation is a small contribution in its own right.

## What remains novel, and how to say it

Taken piece by piece, nothing in the plan is new. Nested polar radii (Alblas, NPS-Net), two contours per instance (Bi-Polar Mask), instance-first part labelling (part-aware panoptic) and soft-area ratio losses (Meng, Kervadec) all exist. What we did not find anywhere is their **combination for densely packed ring-shaped objects**. In that combination, many touching fibers per image are found in one shot, each comes with its axon and its ring already paired and nested, the ring is defined as "fiber minus axon" so the two parts tile the fiber exactly, and training directly targets each fiber's g-ratio. Equally unclaimed is an **evaluation** that scores pairing itself: neither ASTIH nor AxonPath's evaluator checks whether each axon sits in its own ring. Each delta is moderate; together they make a defensible paper if the experiments show a measurable gain.

**Recommended novelty statement:** "Existing axon–myelin pipelines, including multi-head networks such as AxonPath, recover the axon–sheath pairing with post-hoc rules and train only on pixel losses. Nested polar models guarantee containment for a single pre-centred object [Alblas 2022; NPS-Net 2026], and multi-instance two-contour models pair cell and nucleus without guaranteeing containment [Bi-Polar Mask 2022; HSD-WBR 2025]. We present a dense, single-shot decoder for densely packed annular objects in which every predicted fiber carries its axon and myelin ring as a paired, nested partition by construction, train it with a per-instance g-ratio loss under full mask supervision, and introduce a pairing-correctness metric that exposes errors that fiber-detection F1 hides." Do not claim the "first ratio loss", "first nested polar representation", "first joint axon–myelin network" or "first to interpret the inner/outer ratio per ray". Each is refuted above. If the dual-radius head is dropped from the first paper, delete the polar clause and cite Alblas, NPS-Net and Bi-Polar Mask under related work.

## Changes to the two-week plan

The current plan (week 1: evaluator, nnU-Net and Cellpose baselines, oracle pairing; week 2: nested decoder plus g-ratio loss vs nnU-Net) still fits, with four adjustments.

**Add AxonPath as a baseline in week 1.** It is the closest open method and the one a reviewer will ask about. Its released `em_cns` model runs at 8 nm/px, about 3.7 times coarser than the challenge's 2.152 nm/px, so either downscale inputs by about 0.27 or fine-tune with its released training code ([Zenodo 22982902](https://zenodo.org/records/22982902)). Run its own IoC-rule decoder unchanged so the comparison is against AxonPath as published, and report how many inner cylinders its 90% rule drops.

**Make the oracle-pairing experiment the paper's motivating result.** First reproduce ASTIH's TEM1 numbers (0.974/0.924 Dice, 0.791 fiber F1) with the released model. Then decompose the gap two ways. Feeding ground-truth semantic masks (with inter-fiber gaps removed) through the watershed isolates the error the pairing rule causes on its own. Counting merges, splits and pairing violations on real predictions shows how much of the 0.791 is pairing and how much is plain detection. If pairing turns out to be a small share, the headline must move to g-ratio accuracy and containment rather than F1. Knowing that in week 1 is far better than discovering it at review.

**Ship a pairing-correctness metric with the evaluator.** Neither ASTIH nor AxonPath scores it. A simple, defensible definition: among matched fibers, the fraction whose predicted axon also matches the true axon of that same fiber; plus counts of axons with no ring, rings with zero or several axons, and axon pixels touching background. Report it next to fiber F1, and report g-ratio error next to fiber recall, because g-ratio MAE that ignores missed fibers can be gamed by predicting fewer fibers.

**Design the g-ratio loss ablations to survive the obvious objections.** A ratio can be right while both parts are wrong, so pair the ratio term with per-fiber area terms and also report axon diameter and myelin thickness errors. Soft areas equal hard areas only when probabilities are near 0 or 1, so evaluate on post-argmax g-ratio. Run ratio-only vs area-only vs both, size weighting on vs off, all against plain Dice + CE with identical labels, stratified by fiber size. Keep the dual-radius polar head deferred. NPS-Net and Alblas mean it no longer carries the novelty; it would only add a multi-instance generalisation that costs time and risks boundary accuracy.

## Conclusion

The research gap has moved from "nobody models the pair" to "everybody models the parts and glues them with rules". That is a weaker slogan but a stronger paper position, because the glue is now concrete code that can be tested: AxonPath's 90% overlap rule, ADS's centroid watershed, InstanSeg's overwrite-and-match. A method that removes the glue is only convincing if we first show how much the glue costs. That makes the oracle-pairing decomposition and the pairing-correctness metric the load-bearing experiments, not the decoder architecture.

The second shift is that our idea's closest relatives sit outside neuroscience, in eye, heart and vessel imaging, where nested ratios like the cup-to-disc ratio have already been built into both representations and losses for single objects. Framing our contribution as their **many-instance generalisation to densely packed rings**, evaluated with a metric the field lacks, is both honest and harder to dismiss than any "first" claim. The one finding that could still shrink it is sitting behind the Bi-Polar Mask paywall.
