# Pairing, not pixels, limits myelinated-fiber segmentation

*Status as of 10–11 Nov 2026. Challenge facts come from the organisers' site source and take precedence over any secondary source.*

On the problem this challenge poses, pixel accuracy is close to solved and the open problem is pairing. On ASTIH TEM1, the closest public relative of the challenge data (mouse corpus callosum TEM, 2.36 nm/px), a per-dataset nnU-Net reaches **axon Dice 0.974 and myelin Dice 0.924**. Yet the same predictions, once turned into fibers by the standard AxonDeepSeg watershed, give only **fiber F1 = 0.791 at IoU 0.5** ([ASTIH, MIDL 2026](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mlresearch/v315/main/assets/collin26a/collin26a.pdf)). As of November 2026, no published method predicts an axon and its myelin sheath as a jointly parametrised, nested instance pair, and none trains on per-fiber g-ratio error. No public benchmark reports HD95 in nm or a per-fiber g-ratio MAE, so the SIPAIM 2026 metric set has no published reference numbers at all. The prior literature review mostly holds up. Its main errors were challenge facts it could not read, which the site source now settles: **400 images = 266 annotated + 134 unannotated test**, uint16 paired IDs, no 608/157 split and no "~450 unlabeled" pool ([data.md](https://github.com/alexandrojim/tem-challenge/blob/main/content/data.md)). It also stated a few things as fact that do not exist, the main one being a "dual-radius StarDist". Public data overlap with the challenge is **unlikely**: every public set comes from a different lab, microscope and pixel size. A new CC-BY dataset, **AxonPath** (Zenodo, Oct 2026: 20 mouse corpus callosum TEM images at ~2.10 nm/px with fiber instance labels), is now the best external test set. Our recommended path has three steps. First, build a strict evaluator with a declared default for every ambiguity in the metric spec. Second, run five baselines through one shared pairing step. Third, publish a nested-by-construction decoder that adds a differentiable g-ratio loss and a containment prior, together with an analysis showing that the official g-ratio MAE can be gamed by predicting fewer fibers.

## The challenge rewards the step that every published tool improvises

The task is paired instance segmentation of myelinated fibers in **4096 × 3008** single-channel TEM micrographs of mouse corpus callosum. The images were acquired at ICM Paris at 100 kV and 3000× on an AMT NS12 camera, at **2.152 nm/px**, so each frame covers about 8.8 × 6.5 µm. Masks are uint16. Label **0** is background, which includes unmyelinated axons. Label **1** marks artifacts, which are ignored in loss and metrics. Each axon gets an even ID **X ≥ 2**, and its myelin ring gets **X+1** ([data.md](https://github.com/alexandrojim/tem-challenge/blob/main/content/data.md)). Scoring has three parts ([challenge site](https://alexandrojim.github.io/tem-challenge/)). The first is Dice per compartment (all even labels vs all odd labels ≥ 3) plus their mean. The second is HD95 on the axon and myelin boundaries, converted to nm. The third is a per-fiber g-ratio MAE. For it, predicted fibers (X ∪ X+1) are matched to GT fibers by IoU, g is computed as the ratio of equivalent-circle diameters (algebraically √(A_axon/A_fiber)), and the error is averaged over matched fibers only. Teams are ranked by the mean of their ranks on DSC_Mean, HD95_Mean and g-MAE, with ties going to the lower HD95. The organisers name two gaps: generalisation across preparation and imaging, and loss of the axon-in-myelin nesting.

Two facts frame everything below. First, **the test GT is not public**, and we did not register. Every number we produce must come from an internal held-out split of the 266 annotated images, plus external datasets. Second, the stale parts of the site still contradict the authoritative content files. The checklist says "PNG masks {0, 1, 2}"; the repo README gives 80–120 kV and 3.8–5.5 nm/px; and the deadlines differ (13 Nov in `challenge.md`, 14 Nov in `config.js`) ([config.js](https://github.com/alexandrojim/tem-challenge/blob/main/js/config.js)). The commit history shows the challenge began as a 3-class semantic task and was rewritten as a paired-instance task in October. The data come with no stated licence, DOI, animal count or experimental condition ([data.md](https://github.com/alexandrojim/tem-challenge/blob/main/content/data.md)). We therefore do not redistribute them, and we cannot split by animal until the metadata is inspected or the organisers answer.

## Surveys and state of the art: strong semantic models, improvised instances

### Domain methods are semantic-first and pair fibers by heuristics

Every maintained axon/myelin tool still predicts semantic axon and myelin masks and recovers fibers afterwards. **AxonDeepSeg** (ADS; v5.5.0, April 2026; master updated 30 Oct 2026) added a multi-resolution TEM nnU-Net model (`model_seg_tem-multires`, trained on TEM1 + TEM2) in August 2026 and morphometric filtering in September. It still ships no instance model ([ADS releases](https://github.com/axondeepseg/axondeepseg/releases); [model_cards.yaml](https://github.com/axondeepseg/axondeepseg/blob/master/AxonDeepSeg/model_cards.yaml)). Its pairing step uses axon-component centroids as seeds and floods the negative axon distance transform, masked to axon ∪ myelin. Its g-ratio is d_axon,eq / d_fiber,eq, **exactly the challenge's definition** ([compute_morphometrics.py](https://github.com/axondeepseg/axondeepseg/blob/master/AxonDeepSeg/morphometrics/compute_morphometrics.py)). Reading that code exposes the failure modes the challenge will punish. An integer centroid of a crescent-shaped axon can land outside the axon. Myelin between two touching fibers is split by flooding order across a flat basin, because the axon distance transform is zero outside axons, rather than along a real myelin–myelin boundary. A fragmented axon seeds two fibers, and myelin with no seed is dropped. ASTIH's 2 px inter-fiber gap in the GT is what makes this watershed exact on annotations. ASTIH's authors concede that the step "may struggle" in densely packed or pathological tissue ([ASTIH](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mlresearch/v315/main/assets/collin26a/collin26a.pdf)).

**ASTIH** (Collin, Boudreau, Cohen-Adad; MIDL 2026, PMLR 315:3608–3627) is the field's benchmark. It has five datasets and more than 69,000 fibers across TEM, SEM and bright-field. Its baselines are nnU-Net v2.2 semantic models with the ADS watershed, plus a Cellpose model fine-tuned from cyto3 on whole fibers. It evaluates fibers (axon ∪ myelin) only, with StarDist matching at IoU 0.3 and 0.5, and reports **no HD95 and no g-ratio error** ([ASTIH](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mlresearch/v315/main/assets/collin26a/collin26a.pdf)). Its Table 4 is the most useful single result for us. On TEM1 at IoU 0.5, nnU-Net + watershed gets P/R/F1 = 0.747 / 0.851 / 0.791, while Cellpose gets 0.990 / 0.797 / 0.882. That is a mirror-image precision–recall trade-off, which points to a hybrid. On a dense bright-field image, nnU-Net's F1 falls linearly as the IoU threshold rises, while Cellpose's stays flat from 0.3 to 0.7.

The rest of the domain literature fills in the picture. The **optic-nerve dual-decoder nnU-Net** (Ma, Hao et al., *Ophthalmology Science* 2026) is the only published model with separate inner (axon) and outer (fiber) heads. It reached R² > 0.96 for diameters and area but only **R² = 0.805 for g-ratio**, from 368 images of 23 mice. Its stated code repository returns 404 ([PMC13091109](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13091109/)). **AimSeg** (*PLoS Comput Biol* 2023) builds a true fibre ⊃ inner region ⊃ axon hierarchy with ilastik and Fiji. Its g-ratio agreement (CCC 0.75–0.87) lags far behind its fibre-area agreement ([PMC10691719](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10691719/)). That repeats the pattern that small errors on both contours compound in the ratio. **MicroStructFormer** (bioRxiv, v2 July 2026) runs Mask2Former Swin-L for organelles and periaxonal space on top of ADS axon and myelin masks. It pairs them with a myelin-barrier watershed followed by geodesic assignment and reports only distribution-level validation ([bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.05.26.727755v2.full)). **SnapG** (bioRxiv, January 2026) is classical thresholding and contours, not deep learning ([bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.01.14.699516v1.full)). The **EM-SAM** score of "0.984" is semantic mIoU on serial-SEM splenium ([arXiv 2307.02464](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.02464)). Its 2025 Shank3B follow-up needed about four months of manual refinement and made axon and myelin labels separately, with no pairing ([Front. Neuroinform. 2025](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/neuroinformatics/articles/10.3389/fninf.2025.1563799/full)). The NeuroPoly **generalist** model scores only 0.697 / 0.706 Dice on out-of-distribution macaque TEM at 9 nm/px, and its authors judge SAM "not ideal" because it needs a prompt for every object ([arXiv 2409.11552](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.11552)). Searches found **no 2025–2026 MICCAI, ISBI, MIDL, MedIA or TMI paper** on paired or nested myelinated-axon instances or on g-ratio-optimised training, and no public SIPAIM participant papers. The challenge leaderboard ("MyeloVision Net 0.934" and so on) is still flagged as under validation and should be treated as a placeholder ([challenge site](https://alexandrojim.github.io/tem-challenge/)).

### General instance techniques separate touching objects but none nests them

The techniques literature offers good mechanisms for separating touching objects, but almost nothing that keeps a disk inside its ring as two linked instances. **nnU-Net v2 with ResEnc presets** remains the best-validated semantic backbone. *nnU-Net Revisited* (MICCAI 2024) showed that many claimed gains disappear under rigorous validation, and the documentation now recommends ResEnc-L as the default ([arXiv 2404.09556](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.09556); [resenc_presets.md](https://github.com/MIC-DKFZ/nnUNet/blob/master/documentation/resenc_presets.md)). Every Revisited benchmark is 3D CT/MRI, though, so its relevance to 2D TEM is inferred. Its **region-based training** can encode hierarchical classes such as fiber ⊃ axon directly ([region_based_training.md](https://github.com/MIC-DKFZ/nnUNet/blob/master/documentation/region_based_training.md)). **Cellpose 4.2** (June 2026) ships `cpsam_v2` (SAM ViT-L) and DINOv3-based `cpdino`. Cellpose-SAM is still a bioRxiv preprint ([cellpose models.rst](https://github.com/MouseLand/cellpose/blob/main/docs/models.rst); [bioRxiv API](https://api.biorxiv.org/pubs/biorxiv/10.1101/2025.04.28.651001)). Its flows converge to one centre per object, so a ring and the disk inside it cannot become two instances. Its training diameters of 7.5–120 px are also far below our fibers, which are roughly 230–1,400 px across, so heavy downsampling is needed. **μSAM** (*Nature Methods* 22:579–591, 2025) offers AIS (foreground plus centre and boundary distances, then watershed) and a newer APG mode. Its current EM generalist covers roundish organelles, not boundaries or rings ([micro-sam docs](https://github.com/computational-cell-analytics/micro-sam/blob/master/doc/finetuned_models.md)). In the independent MIDL 2026 benchmark, Cellpose-SAM and μSAM-APG lead and SAM3 trails (mSA 0.269 on label-free cells vs 0.544 for Cellpose-SAM). That benchmark contains **no EM data** ([arXiv 2603.17845](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.17845)).

Three lines of work come closest to nesting by construction. **HydraStarDist / HSD-WBR** (arXiv 2504.12078, preprint) predicts nested star-convex sets, such as nuclei inside cells, in one shot. It encourages containment with a soft penalty rather than guaranteeing it ([arXiv 2504.12078](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.12078)). **Part-aware panoptic segmentation** (CVPR 2021) makes parts inherit their parent's instance ID during the merge. That is exactly the (2k, 2k+1) output contract ([arXiv 2106.06351](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.06351)). **Multi-level activation** (ECCV 2018 workshop) gives semantic containment for nested classes ([arXiv 1804.01910](https://arxiv.org/abs/1804.01910)). From connectomics, **affinities with local shape descriptors** ([Nature Methods 2023](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36585455/)) and the **mutex watershed** ([TPAMI 2021](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1904.12654)) partition dense EM without explicit seeds. Neither has been applied to myelinated-axon TEM. On the loss side, **TopoInteraction** (ECCV 2022) turns "axon is contained by myelin" into a rule that axon and background must never be adjacent pixels. It enforces the rule with a 3×3 convolution that costs about 0.8 s per epoch, and it raised IVUS lumen Dice from 0.893 to 0.949 while cutting violations from 2.71% to 0.16% ([arXiv 2207.09654](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.09654)). TopoMortar (BMVC 2025) found that no topology loss wins everywhere. It also found that **augmentation plus self-distillation lifts plain CE+Dice above most topology losses** ([arXiv 2503.03365v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.03365v2)). Boundary loss helps most when the regional loss struggles ([arXiv 1812.07032](https://arxiv.org/abs/1812.07032)), and distance-map methods do not help on every dataset ([Ma et al., MIDL 2020](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v121/ma20b.html)). For EM pretraining, the options are CEM500K and CEM1.5M ([eLife 2021](https://elifesciences.org/articles/65894); [EMPIAR-11035](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/empiar/EMPIAR-11035/)), RETINA ([PMC12143494](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12143494/)), EM-DINO/OmniEM ([bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.04.13.648639v4.full)) and EMCF ([bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2025.12.09.693109v1.full)). None has been evaluated on myelin or across TEM preparation protocols.

### The survey layer is thin and mostly borrowed from adjacent fields

There is **no dedicated survey** of axon/myelin instance segmentation, of topology-aware segmentation in a top venue, or of domain generalisation for EM. A paper should therefore build its related-work section from the surveys and benchmarks below, and treat ASTIH, TopoMortar and the MIDL 2026 foundation-model benchmark as de-facto reviews.

## Public data: AxonPath and TEM1 are the useful neighbours, and neither is the challenge data

Six public resources carry axon and myelin labels that can be converted to the X/X+1 format. No public dataset reproduces the challenge's acquisition. The challenge frame size matches the AMT NanoSprint12 sensor exactly ([datasheet](https://azure.milexia.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/7/2023/03/AMT-NanoSprint12rev5.pdf)). In contrast, every ICM corpus callosum TEM paper we found used a Hitachi HT7700 with 2048 × 2048 AMT XR41-B/41B cameras. Examples are Huré et al. (*Nat Commun* 2024), with donors Ghayad and Hassan ([doi](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-024-54003-9)), and Lousada et al. (*Brain Sci* 2021), which applied AxonDeepSeg to ICM striatal TEM with Nait-Oumesmar and Cohen-Adad ([doi](https://doi.org/10.3390/brainsci11101353)). None of the ASTIH or White Matter Microscopy Database folders comes from Paris. Our verdict is that **data-level overlap is unlikely, with moderate-to-high confidence**. The challenge images are probably recent ICM.Quant acquisitions, likely from LPC or cuprizone remyelination studies, but that is unconfirmed. The Lousada link makes **model-level** contamination conceivable: ICM annotators might have pre-labelled with ADS models trained on TEM1. That would bias the GT style toward ADS conventions without leaking pixels. It is speculative, and the organisers should be asked.

The conversions are simple. The main risk is that **annotation conventions do not match**. For ASTIH, label connected components of axon ∪ myelin, then write axon = 2k and myelin = 2k+1, drop axons that have no myelin, and rescale by 2.36/2.152 ≈ 1.097. For AxonPath, rasterise the full-resolution GeoJSON at 2.105/2.152 ≈ 0.978 and mark pixels outside the ROI as label 1. The open question is the **inner tongue**. AxonPath separates `Axon` from `InnerCylinder`, which is axon plus inner tongue, while the challenge never says on which side of the tongue its axon boundary lies. Our g-ratio sensitivity table (next section) shows why this matters: a convention offset of a few pixels moves g by several hundredths on small fibers, which is larger than many method differences. The first data task is therefore to inspect the 266 training masks for tongue handling, how touching sheaths are split, and the scale-bar label. External scores should be reported under both tongue conventions.

## Five baselines, one shared pairing step

The baselines should isolate a single question: how much of the error comes from the representation, as opposed to the pairing heuristic? To make that separable, every semantic baseline goes through **the same semantic-to-paired-instance module**. That module is an improved ADS-style marker watershed: seeds are axon components (one seed per component, not an integer centroid), flooding runs over a fiber-level distance map, axon holes are filled, and a fiber is dropped unless it has exactly one axon and one ring. Instance-native baselines bypass the watershed but share the same within-fiber axon/myelin labelling and ID writer. Before running anything on challenge data, reproducing ASTIH's TEM1 numbers with our evaluator validates the pipeline against a published reference: Dice 0.974/0.924, and fiber F1@0.5 of 0.791 for nnU-Net and 0.882 for Cellpose.

Two hybrid diagnostics come almost for free and should run as well. The first is **B1 ∪ B3 consensus**: take Cellpose fibers where they exist and fill recall from nnU-Net watershed fibers. The second is an **oracle-pairing run**: apply the pairing step to GT semantic masks with the inter-fiber gaps removed. The oracle run measures how much error pairing alone can introduce, which sets the headroom for the paper's method. A few practical defaults support these runs. Tile at 1024–2048 px with at least 25% overlap and Gaussian blending, and run pairing once on the stitched full-resolution map. Ensemble probabilities and flows rather than instance maps. Apply strong grayscale intensity augmentation (gamma, contrast, CLAHE, histogram matching), the grayscale analogue of TopoMortar's colour augmentation ([arXiv 2503.03365v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.03365v2)), because the organisers name staining and preparation variation as the core generalisation gap.

## A rigorous evaluator: every ambiguity gets a default and a switch

The official spec leaves at least ten implementation choices open, and each one changes the scores. Reinke et al. name exactly these as pitfalls P3.1 (implementation) and P3.2 (aggregation) ([PMC11181963](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11181963/)). Even "HD95" means different things in different libraries. MONAI and DeepMind take the **maximum of the two directed 95th percentiles**, with DeepMind weighting by surface area. panoptica takes the **95th percentile of the pooled distance set**. MONAI returns inf when exactly one mask is empty and NaN when both are ([MONAI](https://github.com/Project-MONAI/MONAI/blob/dev/monai/metrics/hausdorff_distance.py); [surface-distance](https://github.com/google-deepmind/surface-distance/blob/master/surface_distance/metrics.py); [panoptica](https://github.com/BrainLesion/panoptica/blob/main/panoptica/metrics/hausdorff_distance.py)). Matching has one fixed point to build on. Above IoU 0.5 a match is provably unique, so greedy and Hungarian matching agree there ([HoVer-Net stats_utils](https://github.com/vqdang/hover_net/blob/master/metrics/stats_utils.py)). For a non-overlapping label map without confidence scores, Metrics Reloaded recommends assignment at IoU > 0.5 ([PMC11182665](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11182665/)). The table below sets our defaults. Each should be a switch in `configs/default.yaml`, and every headline result should come with a sensitivity table across the switches.

| Ambiguity | Default (official replica) | Switches reported in sensitivity table | Rationale |
|---|---|---|---|

Ambiguity 4 deserves a dedicated experiment in the paper, because the metric can be **gamed**. g-MAE averages over matched fibers only, so a model that emits only its most confident large fibers pays nothing for the fibers it misses. Large fibers are also exactly where g is least sensitive to boundary error. The derivation is short. For an annulus with axon radius r, a 1-pixel outward shift of the axon/myelin interface changes g by a relative amount Δg/g = p/r, independent of g itself (p = 2.152 nm). This is our first-order calculation, checked numerically:

| Axon diameter | Myelin thickness at g = 0.7 | Δg, interface +1 px | Δg, interface +2 px | Δg, both contours +1 px |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.2 µm | 43 nm (≈20 px) | +0.015 | +0.030 | +0.005 |
| 0.5 µm | 107 nm (≈50 px) | +0.006 | +0.012 | +0.002 |
| 1.0 µm | 214 nm (≈100 px) | +0.003 | +0.006 | +0.001 |

Dropping fibers below about 0.3 µm therefore lowers g-MAE, costs a little Dice, and is invisible to the official ranking. Our evaluator should quantify this trade-off with a confidence or size-threshold sweep that plots g-MAE against fiber recall. A related calculation shows that the *definition* of g matters as much as the boundaries. For elliptical profiles with aspect ratio 1.2–2.0, the area-based g and a minor-axis g differ by **0.02–0.07**. So g values should only be compared with published ones that use the same definition, which for ADS and the challenge means area-based.

Beyond the official replica, the evaluator needs three more tiers. **Tier 2 (instance diagnostics)** covers fiber P/R/F1 at IoU 0.5 and 0.75 using StarDist semantics ([matching.py](https://github.com/stardist/stardist/blob/main/stardist/matching.py)), PQ = SQ × RQ, and AJI+ rather than AJI, since AJI over-penalises missed neighbours ([arXiv 1812.06499](https://arxiv.org/html/1812.06499v5)). It also counts merges and splits, and measures the **pairing-violation rate**: axons without a ring, rings with zero or several axons, axon pixels adjacent to background, and rings with β1 ≠ 1. It adds NSD at τ = 2–3 px, the Metrics Reloaded default boundary metric for noisy references; the tolerance is our assumption. Finally, it reports a seam diagnostic: split and merge rates within ±64 px of tile seams. **Tier 3 (morphometry)** gives Bland–Altman bias and 95% limits of agreement for g, axon diameter and myelin thickness, plus ICC(2,1) and Lin's CCC (with R² only as a supplement), stratified by axon diameter (< 0.3, 0.3–0.5, 0.5–0.8, > 0.8 µm). It also checks distribution-level outputs, namely the per-image mean g and the slope of g against axon diameter, because a model can have low MAE and still bias the slope biologists use. **Tier 4 (statistics)** handles the data hierarchy. Fibers within an image are not independent, and neither are images from one animal. Split with 5-fold `GroupKFold` on the best available group key (animal or block from filenames or TIFF metadata, otherwise acquisition-time clusters, otherwise image), and freeze a held-out set of about 53 images (20%) that is never used for tuning. Put CIs on fiber-level statistics with a **cluster bootstrap over images**. Compare methods with paired Wilcoxon signed-rank tests on per-image scores (Friedman plus Holm-corrected pairwise tests for more than two methods) and report effect sizes. Tile-level leakage inflated pathology scores by up to 41% ([Bussola et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1909.06539)), so splits must never be made by tile. With about 53 held-out images, Dice CIs will be narrow and rank CIs wide, so no "beats baseline" claim should rest on a single rank table ([Maier-Hein 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1806.02051)).

## The publishable gap: fibers that are nested by construction and trained on g-ratio

The literature leaves four gaps, and one method plus one analysis can cover them. First, **pairing is post-hoc everywhere**: ASTIH's 18-point gap between pixel Dice and fiber F1 on TEM1 is the measurable cost. Second, **no method guarantees containment**. HydraStarDist and TopoInteraction are soft, and AimSeg is rule-based. Third, **no method optimises a morphometric**. Within our search budget we found no differentiable g-ratio or diameter loss for ring objects, although we cannot rule one out. Fourth, **no benchmark reports pairing-aware or morphometric metrics**, and the official g-MAE has a demonstrable blind spot.

The recommended method is an **instance-first, part-second nested decoder** on an nnU-Net ResEnc-L 2D backbone. It has three heads. A filled-fiber foreground head predicts axon ∪ myelin. A fiber-separation head works on *filled fibers*, never on rings, because a ring's centroid lies in its hole. It can be Cellpose-style flows, HoVer-style distance maps or boundary affinities, chosen by ablation. A within-fiber part head predicts inner vs outer. Decoding first separates fibers, then labels each fiber's pixels as axon (the inner part, holes filled, largest component) or myelin, and writes them as 2k and 2k+1. **Valid pairing and containment then hold by construction**, following the part-aware panoptic recipe ([arXiv 2106.06351](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.06351)). A riskier but more novel variant adds a **dual-radius polar head**: K outer radii r_out(θ), with inner radii constrained as r_in(θ) = σ(·)·r_out(θ). Containment is then exact, and the per-ray ratio is a radial g-ratio profile. Polygon quantisation and non-star-convex oblique profiles threaten HD95, so this head should be refined against the pixel-level part map rather than replace it.

Three training signals target the metrics directly. The first is a **differentiable g-ratio loss**. For each GT fiber k, compute soft areas Â_axon = Σ p_axon and Â_fiber = Σ p_fiber over a slightly dilated GT fiber region, then penalise |√(Â_axon/Â_fiber) − g_k|, weighted toward small fibers where the sensitivity table shows g is fragile. The second is a **TopoInteraction containment term**, under which axon must not touch background. Its d-containment form can also impose a minimum myelin thickness, and it costs about one convolution per step ([arXiv 2207.09654](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.09654)). The third is a **boundary loss on both contours**, ramped in from a small weight because it does not train well alone ([arXiv 1812.07032](https://arxiv.org/abs/1812.07032)). Because TopoMortar found that augmentation plus self-distillation often beats topology losses, those two belong in every arm, including the baseline. The topology term must earn its place in the ablation ([arXiv 2503.03365v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.03365v2)). Self-distillation on the 134 unlabelled test images is transductive. The challenge explicitly allows semi- and self-supervision ([data.md](https://github.com/alexandrojim/tem-challenge/blob/main/content/data.md)), but it should be reported separately and cannot be used on our internal held-out split in a way that touches its labels. Encoder initialisation (ImageNet vs CEM1.5M vs EM-DINO) is a cheap secondary ablation, not the core claim.

The paper should state falsifiable hypotheses. **H1:** pairing, not semantics, dominates instance error. It is confirmed if B1's oracle-pairing headroom exceeds its semantic headroom on fiber F1@0.5. **H2:** by-construction decoding removes pairing violations and raises fiber F1@0.5 over B1 and B3 without losing compartment Dice. **H3:** the g-ratio loss lowers g bias and MAE in the < 0.3 µm bin and flattens the size dependence of g error. It is rejected if the gain disappears once recall is held fixed. **H4:** the gains transfer zero-shot to AxonPath CC TEM and ASTIH TEM1 (after scale and convention harmonisation) better than ADS multires-TEM does. The evaluation contribution can be a standalone short paper: an open, switch-documented metric suite, a sensitivity analysis showing how much each spec ambiguity moves the ranks, and the recall–g-MAE gaming curve. MIDL is the natural venue, since both ASTIH and the foundation-model cell benchmark appeared at MIDL 2026 ([PMLR v315](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v315/collin26a.html)). The main risks are a mismatch in annotation conventions (inner tongue, sheath splitting), which inspecting the training masks resolves before any modelling; the absence of animal IDs, which limits claims to image-level generalisation unless the organisers supply metadata; and the small held-out set, which bootstrap rank stability must address.

## Conclusion

This review mainly changes where the effort should go. A year of rapid progress on foundation models (Cellpose-SAM, cpdino, μSAM APG, SAM3) has not touched the specific structure of this task: a disk inside a ring, sharing one identity. Even ASTIH, the field's newest benchmark, scores fibers rather than pairs and reports no morphometric error. The most defensible contribution is therefore not a stronger encoder. It is a representation in which invalid pairs cannot be produced, trained on the quantity biologists actually use, and evaluated with metrics that cannot be gamed by staying silent on hard fibers.

There is also a methodological point that cuts against the challenge itself. Its g-ratio metric rewards withholding uncertain fibers, its HD95 has at least three legitimate implementations, and its ranking averages two correlated boundary-driven metrics. A team that publishes the evaluator, the sensitivity analysis and the recall-coupled g-ratio protocol alongside a nested-instance method would make results reproducible beyond this one leaderboard. The first concrete step costs nothing: open the 266 training masks and settle the inner-tongue, sheath-splitting and scale-bar conventions before any model is trained.
