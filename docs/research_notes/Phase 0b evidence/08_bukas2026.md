# Bukas et al. 2026: pitfalls of AI-assisted labelling in EM

**Citation:** Bukas C, Engler J, Pelin H, Hagenberg J, Khan H, Campi F, Eberhagen C, Zischka H, Piraud M. "AI-assisted labeling and its pitfalls: A case study in Electron Microscopy segmentation." MICCAI 2026 Workshops (HAIC), LNCS 17279. **Peer-reviewed workshop paper.**
**Local copy:** `docs/references/papers/bukas2026_miccaiw_ai_assisted_labeling_pitfalls_em.pdf` (14.0 MB, fetched with resume) + `bukas2026.txt`. **Read in full** 15 Nov. The OpenReview page needs a login.

## What they did
- **Data:** TEM of rat liver mitochondria (LPP rats, Wilson's disease model): 277 images, 18 animals (8 diseased, 10 healthy), 8,818 mitochondria, damage classes 1–4.
- **Standard workflow:** Cellpose `cyto` pre-labels, then an expert deletes, adds or corrects instances in their tool BATS. The final set mixes three provenances: *untouched* model masks, *corrected* masks and *added* human masks.
- **Proposed mitigation ("harmonised labels"):** take a box around every final mask, prompt **SAM2**, and replace every mask with SAM2's. All instances then share one boundary style. Mean IoU between mixed and harmonised masks is 0.901, and only 10 of 8,818 harmonisation failures were found by inspection.

## Findings
- **Batch effect by provenance:** in eccentricity–circularity plots, untouched, corrected and added masks form **three distinct curves**. The corrected masks lie between the other two. Model masks are rougher (upsampled from 512 px).
  - Harmonising removes the effect. The median shift in circularity was 0.192 (untouched), 0.099 (corrected) and 0.012 (added).
- **Models trained on the labels learn their boundary style.** Fine-tuned Cellpose boundary F1 was 91.8 (mixed) vs 92.2 (harmonised) at 1 px. The difference is small but consistent.
- They report **no other bias** by mitochondria class or metadata after stratifying.

## What it means for us
1. **C3 design:**
   - **Hard ignore (D-18) adds no model-made labels**, so it creates no boundary-style batch effect. That is an argument *for* ignore over pseudo-labelling as the primary C3 arm.
   - Any pseudo-label arm (suggested by BMVC 2021, `03_c3_unread_papers.md`) *would* mix styles. It would need harmonisation, or at least a provenance-stratified morphometry check (circularity and g by label source).
   - Keep "ring evidence first, model only as a filter" for choosing *which* regions to ignore. Score only against human labels.
2. **TEM2 morphometry caveat (C4):** TEM2's labels are corrected AxonDeepSeg pseudo-labels, exactly the mixed-provenance setting. Shape features, and therefore **per-fiber g**, may carry a provenance effect we can't see, because provenance isn't recorded. Treat TEM2 g comparisons as lower confidence. This adds to the C4 report's model-seeding confound, now with a concrete mechanism.
3. **H5 and C1 interpretation:** boundary style is learnable and shifts shape features. Part of a model's g error against one annotator may be *style*, not error. Our human-vs-human g floor (0.0067) is the reference for how much style alone moves g in our data.
4. **Pitfall for any future labelling we do** (for example the T-20 audit or relabelling for B2): either draw all masks by hand, or harmonise them. Don't mix.
